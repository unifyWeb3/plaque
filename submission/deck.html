The problem

A brand-new account holds zero, so it cannot write anything.

On Portaldot a new account starts at 0 tPOT. It cannot send a transaction, claim a name, register for a program or leave any trace on chain.

Testnet POT is handed out by hand through a manual support ticket. So the first experience of the ecosystem is: wait for a human.

And every dApp that wants to grow has the same wall: it must ask people to buy gas before they can use it. The reusable answer is a sponsored write; Plaque makes it a module another dApp can copy.

The chain already ships the fix

MetaTx — fee payment can be delegated, so a signature can be submitted by somebody else.

VerifySignature — the runtime can check a signature natively.

pallet-revive — an EVM, so ordinary Solidity works.

Plaque uses an ordinary sponsor transaction today; MetaTx is roadmap.

The problem2 / 9

The solution

Three steps. The relayer pays.

01

You sign

Pick a handle. Your wallet signs one EIP-191 message. No transaction, no gas, no account funding.

02

A sponsor pays

A relayer — or your own wallet — submits the signed claim and pays the chain's fee. The sponsor is recorded in the event.

03

The contract binds it

It recovers the signer itself and writes the handle to that address. Anyone can read it back from the public RPC.

The digest binds the handle, the claimant, the chain id and the contract — so a signed claim cannot be replayed onto another name, another address, another chain or another contract.

keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32(1), handle, who, 420420777, contract)) → personal_sign

Evidence4 / 9

Built for this chain, measured

The runtime taught us two things. We built to both.

1 · No block values

This EVM reverts on the BLOCK-family opcodes — CHAINID, TIMESTAMP, NUMBER. Two probe contracts in the repo prove it with reverted transactions.

So no deployed build reads a block value: the chain id is a constant, and the digest binds it instead.

2 · Deposits, not just gas

signature check + compute0.028 tPOT
storage deposit per new entry3.84 tPOT
code deposit (302 bytes)36.7 tPOT

786 bytes cost 74.5 tPOT to deploy, which is why the live build is PlaqueLite.sol: 302 bytes, same digest and storage slot; the lite event is the three-address form, differential-tested against the readable build.

Engineering5 / 9

Trust model

The contract trusts nothing but the signature.

No custody

No escrow, no bond, no arbitration, no mint. A claim carries value 0; the lite fallback is payable but has no withdraw, so do not send tPOT to the contract. The only value that moves is the chain’s own deposit, paid by the sponsor to the runtime.

An untrusted sponsor

It can refuse to pay, reorder claims or go offline. It cannot forge a claim, and it cannot bind a name to somebody else’s address.

A dropped claim is recoverable

The claim is a signed message, not a secret. Anybody can resubmit it — including the claimant with their own gas.

A name stays bound in contract state, and the registry is readable by any app from the public RPC.

Known limit, stated openly: a free name can be squatted by binding it to an address the squatter controls. Here that costs ≈3.87 tPOT per name — expensive enough to deter, and a claim-then-confirm grace period would remove it.

Trust6 / 9

Who it is for

A reusable rail, with a registry as its first consumer.

Users. The ~168 hackers in this event, ecosystem newcomers, and anyone who wants a name on chain before they have funds.

Builders. Any Portaldot dApp can reuse the sponsored-write path instead of asking users to buy gas first. docs/INTEGRATE.md is the under-10-minute path: signature verification, policy caps, nonce queue, config and a reader.

The ecosystem. The repository documents the chain's real cost and opcode constraints with transactions — facts every builder here will hit.

The business

The sponsor is the customer: a hosted relayer with a per-developer budget, settled in the chain's native Assets pallet so sponsorship itself is on chain.

Unit economics are measured, not guessed: 3.87 tPOT per sponsored write on this testnet, dominated by the storage deposit.

The registry is the reference consumer; the reusable artifact is the sponsored-write rail. The integration guide is tested against a local chain with refusals and positive controls.
